# Postnatal / women's health / dermatofunctional physiotherapy service pages: structure, trust and booking patterns (research notes, Oct 2026)

Scope note: notes are based on pages fetched directly (marked "fetched") and on search-result summaries (marked "search summary", lower confidence: these were not opened in full). All URLs exact as returned. Researched 2026-10-30.

## 1. Which sections do top postnatal / women's-health physio pages include, and in what order?

### Takeaway
The strongest pages (ID Feminino Lisboa, Ten Health London, PhysioLogic NYC, The Mummy MOT) follow roughly this order: hero with service name + one reassuring line + CTA → "who is this for" symptom list → what the service is → when to come (6 weeks vaginal / 8 weeks C-section, but "years later" is fine too) → what the first assessment involves (with consent language) → how follow-up works → who treats you / credentials → price or how to pay → FAQ → booking block + location. Weak pages (VivaFisio, Nuffield The Holly, Purafisio) skip the assessment walkthrough, FAQ and clinician, and read as thin.

### Inferences
- Section order suggested for Fisio4you (pt-PT), based on the examples above: (1) Hero: "Recuperação pós-parto" / "Fisioterapia pós-parto", one calm line, "Marcar consulta" (WhatsApp) + "Ligar" → (2) "É para si se…": a short checklist of symptoms in plain language → (3) What diastasis is and what postnatal recovery covers (abdominal wall, pelvic floor, C-section/episiotomy scar, posture, return to exercise), with one simple diagram → (4) When to come: around 6 weeks after a vaginal birth or 8 weeks after a C-section, earlier if in pain, and "também se o parto foi há anos" → (5) "Como é a primeira consulta": numbered steps with consent and comfort details → (6) "Como funciona o acompanhamento": phases, and no fixed number of sessions → (7) Who treats you: photo, name, cédula profissional → (8) Scar care as its own short block → (9) FAQ (8–12 questions, modelled on ID Feminino's list) → (10) Booking block with WhatsApp, phone, address and map, plus ERS/complaints-book footer.
- The examples suggest that a named "check" product (Mummy MOT; "revisão pós-parto" in PT; "valoración postparto" in ES) is easier to book than a vague "tratamento". A clearly named, time-boxed first appointment ("Avaliação pós-parto") gives women something concrete to ask for on WhatsApp.

## 2. How do pages explain diastasis / postnatal recovery visually, and do they offer self-checks or symptom checklists?

### Takeaway
None of the sampled clinic pages relied on detailed anatomical diagrams. Most use soft lifestyle photography of women (often pregnant or holding a baby) plus text checklists. Diagrams appear mainly in NHS patient leaflets. Self-checks are usually given as thresholds ("2 fingerbreadths + doming → see a physio") rather than step-by-step tests on clinic pages. The common trust devices are symptom checklists and "real-time ultrasound".

### Cited Findings
- ID Feminino uses only two photos of women (hero and mid-page) and no diagrams or infographics. The 10 symptom subsections do the explanatory work — [ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/)
- PhysioLogic's imagery: a hero photo of an expectant mother "in a black top, cradling her belly beside a sheer curtain", and a photo of "a clinician's hand on a pregnant patient's belly while she reclines on a sofa", plus line icons for 6 benefit cards. No diagrams — [PhysioLogic](https://physiologicnyc.com/physical-therapy-brooklyn-nyc/prenatal-postpartum-care/)
- Sydney Physio Clinic's only visual is a featured image named "Diastasis_Recti.jpg" (contents not described in the page text). Its self-check is prose only: the gap "can be especially apparent when performing a crunch-like sit-up movement". Signs listed: doming/bulging, core weakness, pelvic floor dysfunction, lower back pain — [Sydney Physio Clinic](https://www.sydneyphysioclinic.com.au/womens-health-physio/diastasis-recti/)
- Sydney Physio Clinic reframes the aesthetic question ("How Do You Flatten Your Stomach With Diastasis Recti?") as restoring internal support rather than appearance. It mentions surgery (abdominoplasty) only as a last resort for severe, persistent cases, and says healing may occur within the first 8 weeks postpartum — [Sydney Physio Clinic](https://www.sydneyphysioclinic.com.au/womens-health-physio/diastasis-recti/)
- NHS Borders patient leaflet: "a gap of about one to two fingerbreadths is common… This is not a problem and will usually heal within 6-8 weeks following the delivery of your baby". Seek follow-up if "the gap is wider than 2 fingerbreadths and you have a visible 'doming' of your tummy when you sit up". It includes one image of abdominal separation and exercises, but no step-by-step self-check — [NHS Right Decisions / NHS Borders](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/patient-information-leaflets/primary-community-services/physiotherapy/separation-of-the-abdominal-muscles-in-pregnancy-and-beyond)
- Other NHS leaflets (search summary): Cardiff & Vale tells readers to count fingers in the gap and keep monitoring until it is 2 fingers or fewer, and says closure usually happens within 12 weeks ([Cardiff & Vale UHB](https://cavuhb.nhs.wales/files/maternity-services/separation-of-abdominal-muscles-pdf/)). NHS Lothian treats a 2-finger postnatal gap as normal ([NHS Lothian DRAM guidelines](https://apps.nhslothian.scot/files/sites/2/DRAM-LOTHIAN-PHYSIOTHERAPY-GUIDELINES-April-2017.pdf)). A US hospital blog describes the classic lying test (knees bent, fingers about 3 inches above the navel, lift the head) with a 1.5-finger threshold, which conflicts with the NHS 2-finger threshold ([Spencer Hospital](https://spencerhospital.org/blog/health-wellness/diastasis-recti-mummy-tummy-rehab))
- Symptom checklists are the main "self-recognition" device: ID Feminino has 10 symptom headings ([ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/)), Ten has 10 reasons to attend ([Ten](https://www.ten.co.uk/services/physiotherapy/postnatal-physiotherapy)), PhysioLogic has a 7-item "Commonly Helps With" list ([PhysioLogic](https://physiologicnyc.com/physical-therapy-brooklyn-nyc/prenatal-postpartum-care/)), and VivaFisio lists symptoms (abdominal, lumbar or gluteal pain, incontinence, pain during sex, bowel changes, reduced abdominal strength, prolapse) and risk factors (large bump, multiple pregnancy, age over 35, twins, larger-than-average babies) ([VivaFisio](https://vivafisio.pt/revisao-e-recuperacao-pos-parto/))
- Real-time ultrasound is used as a visual and technical trust cue. Sydney Physio Clinic offers "real-time ultrasound imaging when appropriate" to assess core activation and track progress ([Sydney Physio Clinic](https://www.sydneyphysioclinic.com.au/womens-health-physio/diastasis-recti/)). Pogo Physio offers it as an alternative to an internal exam, while noting it is "a less reliable way to measure strength" ([Pogo Physio](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/blog/womens-health-physiotherapy-consult/)). A Lisbon physio is listed as doing "ecografia funcional abdominal" for diastasis (search summary, [Centro do Bebé – Raquel Jacinto](https://centrodobebe.pt/especialistas/raquel-jacinto/))
- Ten uses a lay synonym alongside the clinical term: "Over 30% will have diastasis recti (also known as the tummy gap)" — [Ten](https://www.ten.co.uk/services/physiotherapy/postnatal-physiotherapy)

### Inferences
- For a discreet audience, the safest visual approach seen across the examples is: (a) one clean line-drawing diagram of the abdominal wall (rectus muscles, linea alba, gap, with no real bodies); (b) photography of the treatment room, the clinician and a woman with a baby, all clothed; (c) a symptom checklist styled as tickable items. No real abdomens, no before/after photos (see Q5 for the legal reasons).
- A self-check box can be offered as orientation, not diagnosis: "uma separação de 1–2 dedos é comum nas primeiras semanas; se for maior que 2 dedos ou notar um 'cume' na barriga ao levantar a cabeça, vale a pena avaliar". It should cite the NHS-style threshold and point to the assessment. Phrase it cautiously because thresholds differ between sources (1.5 vs 2 fingers).
- Fisio4you must not claim real-time ultrasound unless the clinic actually has it (memory rule: confirmed facts only).

### Gaps
- I could not see the actual images (WebFetch returns only filenames and alt text), so claims about how "warm" or "clinical" the photography looks are limited to described alt text.
- I found no clinic page with an interactive self-check tool or quiz.

## 3. Tone and privacy cues: how do pages reassure (female clinician, private room, what to wear, bringing the baby, consent)?

### Takeaway
The best reassurance language deals with the internal exam (optional, explained beforehand, consent-based, can stop at any time, chaperone available, shouldn't hurt) and with not needing to "know" what you need. "Bring your baby" appears explicitly on Australian pages but on none of the Portuguese pages sampled. "What to wear" and "female clinician" were almost never stated. These gaps are easy wins for differentiation.

### Cited Findings
- ID Feminino on the internal exam: "Esta avaliação é explicada previamente, depende do consentimento da mulher e pode ser interrompida a qualquer momento." and "A avaliação não deve provocar dor." It reassures on the decision itself: "Não precisa de saber antecipadamente qual é o tratamento de que necessita." It also mentions a "reservado" environment and respect for each woman's limits, but does not state clinician gender, bringing the baby, or what to wear — [ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/)
- Ten: "An internal examination may be recommended, this is optional and only carried out with your consent", you can request a chaperone, and a "contact us" link invites confidential conversations. Bringing the baby and what to wear are not mentioned — [Ten](https://www.ten.co.uk/services/physiotherapy/postnatal-physiotherapy)
- Pogo Physio: "Don't stress – you can bring bub!" and "If you bub loves to be held, you can perform majority of your physiotherapy consult whilst holding your baby." The internal exam is done "covered with a towel", there is an alternative if you're not comfortable, and "Your physiotherapist will make you feel very comfortable." — [Pogo Physio](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/blog/womens-health-physiotherapy-consult/)
- A Sydney practice advertises a physio gym session where you can bring your baby, plus real-time ultrasound (search summary of a directory listing, details possibly dated) — [Ellaslist – Physio Posture Fitness](https://www.ellaslist.com.au/sydney/directory/services/physio-posture-fitness-phsyiotherapy-pilates-prenatal-postnatal-fitness)
- Female clinician: VivaFisio implies it through grammar ("uma fisioterapeuta especializada em saúde da mulher") without stating it ([VivaFisio](https://vivafisio.pt/revisao-e-recuperacao-pos-parto/)). PhysioLogic's postpartum page profiles two male Doctors of Physical Therapy ([PhysioLogic](https://physiologicnyc.com/physical-therapy-brooklyn-nyc/prenatal-postpartum-care/)). Physiokinesis names no clinician, only a "Fisioterapeuta especializado na Saúde da Mulher" ([Physiokinesis](https://www.physiokinesis.pt/fisioterapia-e-saude-da-mulher))
- Credentials as reassurance: Ten has a "Chartered & Regulated" block (CSP, HCPC) ([Ten](https://www.ten.co.uk/services/physiotherapy/postnatal-physiotherapy)). The Mummy MOT says "All our practitioners are fully trained in postnatal care and licensed to carry out a Mummy MOT®" ([The Mummy MOT](https://www.themummymot.com/)). ID Feminino says "com formação na saúde da mulher" ([ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/))
- Honest-expectation lines that also reassure: "Sempre que necessário, é recomendada avaliação médica complementar." and "Não existe um número igual para todas as mulheres." (number of sessions); return to sport depends on recovery, not on time elapsed — [ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/)
- Gentle framing of the postpartum period: Physiokinesis calls it emotionally and hormonally demanding and offers help "de uma forma segura e consciente" ([Physiokinesis](https://www.physiokinesis.pt/fisioterapia-e-saude-da-mulher)). Purafisio: "O seu bem-estar começa aqui." and "Nós podemos ajudar!" ([Purafisio](https://www.purafisio.pt/fisioterapia-gravidas/))
- Practical-life advice signals that the service understands new mothers: "Education and advice on safe activities such as lifting your baby or pushing a buggy" ([The Mummy MOT](https://www.themummymot.com/)); "Safe lifting, carrying and feeding positions" ([Ten](https://www.ten.co.uk/services/physiotherapy/postnatal-physiotherapy)); "posture for baby care and breastfeeding" ([Physiokinesis](https://www.physiokinesis.pt/fisioterapia-e-saude-da-mulher)); advice on handling and feeding the baby ([ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/))

### Inferences
- A dedicated "Antes de vir" / "O que esperar" block would be a strong differentiator in Portugal, since none of the 4 Portuguese pages sampled had one. It could say: duration; that the baby can come (if true for Fisio4you); comfortable clothing; a private room with the door closed; any internal exam is optional, explained first and needs her consent, and she can stop at any time; she can bring someone with her. Only include items the clinic confirms (memory rule).
- If the women's health clinician at Fisio4you is a woman (the repo notes say Angela is a physiotherapist), stating "a consulta é feita pela fisioterapeuta [nome]" with a photo is a strong discreet cue. Confirm with the clinic who actually does the postnatal sessions before writing it.
- Tone that fits the brand's clinical/institutional voice: short factual statements ("A avaliação não deve provocar dor."), with no exclamation marks and no "recuperar a sua figura" (see Q5).

### Gaps
- None of the fetched pages said what to wear. I found no source with a standard wording.
- Whether women in Portugal specifically prefer a female clinician for this service: no survey data found.

## 4. What does "dermatofuncional" mean in Portugal/Brazil vs. how the UK/AU/US frame the same services, so content is correct for a pt-PT audience?

### Takeaway
"Fisioterapia dermatofuncional" is mainly a Brazilian construct: it is a recognised specialty there (COFFITO) covering skin and tissue dysfunction, scars, and pre/post-plastic-surgery care, often with an aesthetic tilt. In Portugal it is a recent, little-established niche. The Portuguese postnatal pages sampled frame this work as "Fisioterapia Pós-Parto", "Saúde da Mulher" or "Fisioterapia Pélvica", with scar care as a sub-item. The UK/AU/US equivalent is "women's health / pelvic health physiotherapy", "postnatal check / Mummy MOT", "DRAM / diastasis recti", and "C-section scar massage / scar therapy".

### Cited Findings
- Brazil: COFFITO Resolução 362/2009 recognised "Fisioterapia Dermato-Funcional" as a specialty, and Resolução 394/2011 regulates it (search summary; secondary sources) — [Resolução COFFITO 394/2011 (normaslegais)](https://www.normaslegais.com.br/legislacao/resolucao-coffito-394-2011.htm); [Resolução 394/2011 (normasbrasil)](https://www.normasbrasil.com.br/norma/resolucao-394-2011_114256.html)
- Brazil also has a separate women's health specialty. Resolução COFFITO 401/2011 sets the title "Especialista Profissional em Fisioterapia na Saúde da Mulher" (search summary) — [Resolução COFFITO 401/2011](https://www.normasbrasil.com.br/norma/resolucao-401-2011_114236.html)
- Portugal: an IPL (Lisbon) master's thesis describes the "Fisioterapeuta Dermatofuncional" as working "na reabilitação das disfunções tegumentares estético-funcionais e pré e pós cirúrgicos de cirurgia plástica e reconstrutiva", and notes the area is recent and still struggles to become established in Portugal (search summary) — [Repositório IPL](https://repositorio.ipl.pt/handle/10400.21/4811)
- Portuguese clinical framing of C-section scar work: a UFP (Porto) review on physiotherapy after cesarean lists soft-tissue mobilisation, kinesio taping plus exercise and "indução miofascial na cicatriz", with benefits on pain and scar structure (search summary) — [UFP repository](https://bdigital.ufp.pt/entities/publication/b212c936-dfa4-4498-9133-0ff8a3b8b6fb)
- Vocabulary on the Portuguese pages sampled: "Fisioterapia Pós-Parto", "pavimento pélvico", "diástase abdominal", "cicatriz de cesariana" / "episiotomia ou laceração" ([ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/)); "Fisioterapia e Saúde da Mulher", "diástase", "cicatriz (cesariana ou perineal)", "ginástica abdominal hipopressiva" ([Physiokinesis](https://www.physiokinesis.pt/fisioterapia-e-saude-da-mulher)); "Revisão e recuperação pós-parto" ([VivaFisio](https://vivafisio.pt/revisao-e-recuperacao-pos-parto/)); "diástase dos retos abdominais" ([Purafisio](https://www.purafisio.pt/fisioterapia-gravidas/)); "fisioterapia pélvica… (cicatrizes, diástase abdominal, incontinências...)" (search summary, [CUF](https://www.cuf.pt/noticias/disponiveis-tratamentos-especializados-de-fisioterapia)). None of these postnatal pages used "dermatofuncional".
- UK framing: "Postnatal Physiotherapy", "Pelvic Health Specialist", "Women's Health Physiotherapists", "DRAM checks", "tummy gap" ([Ten](https://www.ten.co.uk/services/physiotherapy/postnatal-physiotherapy)); "Mummy MOT®" as a branded, licensed postnatal check ([The Mummy MOT](https://www.themummymot.com/)); NHS leaflets use "Diastasis of the Rectus Abdominus Muscles (DRAM)" / "separation of the abdominal muscles" ([NHS Right Decisions](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/patient-information-leaflets/primary-community-services/physiotherapy/separation-of-the-abdominal-muscles-in-pregnancy-and-beyond))
- AU framing: "Women's Health Physiotherapy", "postnatal review", "real-time ultrasound" ([Pogo Physio](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/blog/womens-health-physiotherapy-consult/); [Sydney Physio Clinic](https://www.sydneyphysioclinic.com.au/womens-health-physio/diastasis-recti/)). US framing: "Prenatal & Postpartum Therapy", "pelvic floor therapy", "post-cesarean rehabilitation" ([PhysioLogic](https://physiologicnyc.com/physical-therapy-brooklyn-nyc/prenatal-postpartum-care/))
- Spain framing: "fisioterapia de suelo pélvico", "valoración postparto", "diástasis abdominal". Quirónsalud's unit has a "Consulta de Valoración Postparto: necesaria para valorar tu funcionalidad tras el parto y determinar si debes o no volver a la actividad física y en qué forma." (search summary) — [Quirónsalud Valle del Henares PDF](https://www.quironsalud.com/valle-henares/es/cartera-servicios/rehabilitacion-medicina-deportiva-fisioterapia/unidad-suelo-pelvico.ficheros/3177173-Unidad%20Suelo%20P%C3%A9lvico.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a pt-PT audience, lead with "Fisioterapia pós-parto" / "Recuperação pós-parto" and "Saúde da mulher". Use "diástase abdominal" (with a lay gloss such as "separação dos músculos da barriga"), "pavimento pélvico" (pt-PT; avoid the Brazilian "assoalho pélvico") and "cicatriz de cesariana". If Fisio4you wants to keep "dermatofuncional" (because the clinic uses it), use it as a secondary label ("cuidado da cicatriz / fisioterapia dermatofuncional") and explain it in one line. Do not use it as the page title, because Portuguese mothers are unlikely to search for it and it carries aesthetic (Brazilian) connotations.
- "Revisão pós-parto" / "Avaliação pós-parto" is the pt-PT counterpart to "Mummy MOT". "Mummy MOT" is a trademark (®) with licensed practitioners, so Fisio4you should not use that name.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas (Portugal) recognises a "saúde da mulher" or "dermatofuncional" specialty title. The only related result was the amendment to the Ordem's statute ([Lei 71/2023, DRE](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei/71-2023-229915927)), which does not address specialties. APFisio and POGP pages were not reached within budget.
- I did not verify the primary COFFITO texts; the Brazilian findings rely on secondary sources.

## 5. CTA placement and booking patterns (WhatsApp, phone, online), plus Portuguese health-advertising constraints

### Takeaway
Good pages repeat a dual CTA (instant booking + a low-commitment "talk to us first") in 3–4 places: header, hero, after the symptoms or assessment section, and the closing block. Portuguese clinics mix phone, WhatsApp, email and forms; WhatsApp appears as an icon or link rather than as the primary button. Portuguese law (DL 238/2015) bans guarantees of results, unevidenced technical claims, inducing consumption without prior individual assessment, and exploiting a vulnerable circumstance. Postnatal pages should therefore avoid "results" language and before/after imagery, and should show the ERS registration.

### Cited Findings
- PhysioLogic: "Schedule Appointment" (booking page) and "Request A Call" (contact form) appear together in the header, hero and closing banner, "Request A Call" again above the FAQ, and "Book an Evaluation" after the overview — [PhysioLogic](https://physiologicnyc.com/physical-therapy-brooklyn-nyc/prenatal-postpartum-care/)
- ID Feminino: "Fazer Marcação" in the header, "Pedir Mais Informações" twice (hero and after pricing, scrolling to the form), phone and email links in the header and contacts, and "Ver Preçário" six times. The enquiry form asks where (Gabinete/Domicílio/Online) and when (Manhã 8h–13h / Tarde 13h–18h / Noite 18h–22h). There is a WhatsApp heading at the end but no working WhatsApp link — [ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/)
- Physiokinesis: booking form "FAÇA AQUI A SUA MARCAÇÃO" ("Preencha este formulário e entraremos em contacto consigo") with a service selector, time slots from 08:00 to 19:00 and a privacy checkbox, then "Mas se preferir pode ligar", plus a WhatsApp link. The footer shows the ERS certificate number and the complaints book ("livro de reclamações"). The phone number shown differs from the one in the link — [Physiokinesis](https://www.physiokinesis.pt/fisioterapia-e-saude-da-mulher)
- Purafisio: hero buttons "Agendar já" and "Saber mais", then "Agendamento Online", phone, WhatsApp, email and "Ligue agora para saber como!" (for health-insurance reimbursement via "regime livre") — [Purafisio](https://www.purafisio.pt/fisioterapia-gravidas/)
- Ten: "Make an enquiry →" twice (hero and after the overview), "Book Postnatal Assessment →" (timetable) after the "why" section, and an enquiry form where the user chooses to be contacted by telephone or email — [Ten](https://www.ten.co.uk/services/physiotherapy/postnatal-physiotherapy)
- The Mummy MOT: a "Find a local Practitioner" directory, plus phone 0330 118 0910 with opening hours. The header "Book Appointment" button links to "#" (broken) — [The Mummy MOT](https://www.themummymot.com/)
- Sydney Physio Clinic: "Book Now" in the header, "BOOK ONLINE NOW" in the footer, and a closing paragraph inviting readers to call — [Sydney Physio Clinic](https://www.sydneyphysioclinic.com.au/womens-health-physio/diastasis-recti/)
- Price display varies: Nuffield shows "Mummy MOT" £120 ([Nuffield The Holly](https://www.nuffieldhealth.com/physiotherapy/the-holly/mummy-mot)); ID Feminino names session types but links out for prices ([ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/)); a Spanish hospital leaflet lists a first pelvic-floor assessment at €65 and a session at €50 (search summary; currency of figures unclear) ([HM Hospitales tríptico](https://docs3.hmhospitales.com/app/uploads/sites/52/2026/07/24150002/WEB-TRIPTICO-11x21cm_SUELO-PELVICO.pdf)); Doctoralia Madrid lists €50–120 per postpartum session (search summary) ([Doctoralia Madrid](https://www.doctoralia.es/tratamientos-servicios/fisioterapia-del-postparto/madrid))
**Portuguese legal constraints, Decreto-Lei 238/2015 (primary text read from the Diário da República PDF)**:
  - Art. 4.º/1: advertising must identify the provider "de forma verdadeira, completa e inteligível". Art. 4.º/3: it is illicit if the provider is not "devidamente registado na Entidade Reguladora da Saúde" — [DL 238/2015 (PDF via OMD)](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/decreto-lei-238-2015-publicidade-saude.pdf)
  - Art. 5.º/3: the message "não deve conter expressões, conceitos, testemunhos ou afirmações que possam criar no utente expectativas potenciadoras de perigo ou potencialmente ameaçadoras para a sua integridade física ou moral" — [DL 238/2015](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/decreto-lei-238-2015-publicidade-saude.pdf)
  - Art. 6.º: "apenas devem ser utilizadas informações aceites pela comunidade técnica ou científica" — [DL 238/2015](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/decreto-lei-238-2015-publicidade-saude.pdf)
  - Art. 7.º/1 prohibits, among other things: (a) "menções de natureza técnica e científica sem suporte de evidência" or "expressões de inovação ou de pioneirismo, sem prévia avaliação"; (b) advertising that "aconselhem ou incitem à aquisição de atos e serviços de saúde, sem atender aos requisitos da necessidade… ou a necessidade de avaliação ou de diagnóstico individual prévio"; (c) advertising that "se refiram falsamente a demonstrações ou garantias de cura ou de resultados ou sem efeitos adversos ou secundários"; (f) "grátis"/"com desconto"/"promoção" claims when the user must pay more than the unavoidable cost — [DL 238/2015](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/decreto-lei-238-2015-publicidade-saude.pdf)
  - Art. 7.º/2(b): prohibits advertising "suscetíveis de induzir o utente ao consumo desnecessário, nocivo ou sem diagnóstico ou avaliação prévios por profissional habilitado". Art. 7.º/3(c) lists as an aggravating aspect "o aproveitamento consciente pelo profissional de qualquer infortúnio ou circunstância específica que pela sua gravidade prejudique a capacidade de decisão do utente" — [DL 238/2015](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/decreto-lei-238-2015-publicidade-saude.pdf)
  - Art. 8.º fines: €250–3,740.98 (pessoa singular) or €1,000–44,891.81 (pessoa coletiva) for breaches of arts. 3–7. Negligence is punishable, with the limits halved. Accessory sanctions include a ban on the activity or advertising for up to 2 years. The ERS supervises and applies the fines — [DL 238/2015](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/decreto-lei-238-2015-publicidade-saude.pdf)
  - ERS Regulamento 1058/2016 complements the decree; Art. 10.º of the DL delegates the identification elements to the ERS (search summary) — [OMD summary of Regulamento ERS 1058/2016](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/informacao-regulamento-ers-1058-2016.pdf); ERS leaflet on advertising practices (not fetchable; DNS error) — [ERS tríptico](https://ers.pt/media/x3enq5ls/triptico_praticas-publicitarias-em-saude_web.pdf)
- Examples of Portuguese wording at risk under these rules: Physiokinesis uses "permitindo recuperar a sua figura", "A Fisioterapia é o tratamento de eleição na Incontinência Urinária Feminina" and "Previne e trata a incontinência urinária", strong affirmative claims with no supporting evidence on the page ([Physiokinesis](https://www.physiokinesis.pt/fisioterapia-e-saude-da-mulher)). Ten's "Most postnatal conditions improve within 4 to 6 sessions" would also be a borderline results claim in Portugal ([Ten](https://www.ten.co.uk/services/physiotherapy/postnatal-physiotherapy)). By contrast, ID Feminino promises no outcomes ("Não existe um número igual para todas as mulheres") ([ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/))
- Evidence context supporting cautious claims: a Spanish university final-year thesis (TFG) concludes physiotherapy is beneficial for postpartum diastasis but there is no consensus on the best exercise programme (search summary) — [UVa TFG](https://uvadoc.uva.es/handle/10324/38782)

### Inferences
- Pattern for Fisio4you (WhatsApp + phone only): the primary button is "Marcar consulta", opening WhatsApp with a pre-filled, neutral message such as "Olá, gostaria de marcar uma avaliação pós-parto." The secondary is "Ligar" (tel:). Add a soft third option, "Tenho uma dúvida", via WhatsApp for women not ready to book. Place the pair in the hero, after "Como é a primeira consulta", after the FAQ, and as a sticky bar on mobile. The pre-filled text should be generic, without symptoms, because WhatsApp previews show on lock screens; this is a discretion point inferred, not sourced.
- Compliance checklist for the page: show the clinic's legal identity and ERS registration number plus the complaints book (Physiokinesis does this in the footer). No "garantido" or "resultados em X sessões". No before/after photos or testimonials that promise outcomes. No "grátis" first assessment unless it is truly free. No fear-based copy aimed at vulnerable postpartum women (art. 5.º/3, 7.º/3(c)). State that a plan follows an individual assessment (art. 7.º/1(b)).
- Patient photos and testimonials involve health data: explicit written consent under GDPR would be needed in addition to DL 238/2015. I did not verify this in this research round, so treat it as a point to confirm.

### Gaps
- The ERS leaflet and good-practice manual (https://www.ers.pt/media/e50by3ag/manual-boas-praticas-publicidade-saude.pdf) could not be fetched (DNS error on ers.pt), so I could not quote the ERS's specific guidance on testimonials or before/after images. DL 238/2015 itself does not mention "antes e depois" explicitly; the restriction follows from art. 7.º (misleading or results guarantees).
- I found no data on WhatsApp conversion rates for health bookings in Portugal.

## 6. Concrete example pages (Portugal, Spain, UK, Australia, USA) with what each does well or badly

### Takeaway
The best single template for Fisio4you is ID Feminino (Lisbon) for structure and consent wording, combined with Ten (London) for the "why come even without symptoms" logic and the chaperone/consent FAQ, Pogo Physio (AU) for the bring-baby warmth, and PhysioLogic (NYC) for the repeated dual-CTA rhythm. VivaFisio and Nuffield The Holly are counter-examples.

### Cited Findings
**Portugal**
- ID Feminino, Lisboa: the most complete pt-PT postnatal page. It has a 10-symptom "Como pode ajudar" section, an assessment list, consent wording for the internal exam, home and online options, and a 13-question FAQ (including IRS deduction and insurance). Weak points: the testimonials section is empty and the WhatsApp heading has no link — [ID Feminino](https://idfeminino.pt/servicos/fisioterapia/pos-parto/)
- Physiokinesis, Lisboa: an umbrella "Saúde da Mulher" page with 5 tiles. Good: clear timing (6 weeks vaginal / 8 weeks C-section), scar and diastasis items, ERS certificate and complaints book in the footer, WhatsApp plus form plus phone. Bad: aesthetic and absolute claims ("recuperar a sua figura", "tratamento de eleição") — [Physiokinesis](https://www.physiokinesis.pt/fisioterapia-e-saude-da-mulher)
- Purafisio, Matosinhos: a combined pregnancy and postpartum page. Good: "6ª/8ª semana" timing, insurance reimbursement note, many contact channels including WhatsApp. Bad: no assessment description, scar section or FAQ — [Purafisio](https://www.purafisio.pt/fisioterapia-gravidas/)
- VivaFisio: a counter-example. It names the "consulta de revisão pós-parto" well but is otherwise thin, with phone only, no address and no FAQ — [VivaFisio](https://vivafisio.pt/revisao-e-recuperacao-pos-parto/)
- Centro do Bebé, Raquel Jacinto: a clinician-profile model (pelvic physio across Lisbon and Porto units, with functional abdominal ultrasound for diastasis) (search summary) — [Centro do Bebé](https://centrodobebe.pt/especialistas/raquel-jacinto/)

**United Kingdom**
- Ten Health & Fitness, London: the best "why book even without symptoms" argument. It has the optional internal exam plus chaperone language, a CSP/HCPC credential block, a 6-question FAQ and a "tummy gap" lay term — [Ten](https://www.ten.co.uk/services/physiotherapy/postnatal-physiotherapy)
- The Mummy MOT (national brand): the clearest description of what the check includes (postural screen, pelvic floor, abdominal separation, bladder/bowel/sexual screening, lifting-baby advice, written report). Bookings go through a practitioner directory and phone — [The Mummy MOT](https://www.themummymot.com/)
- Nuffield Health, The Holly: a counter-example. The Mummy MOT URL lands on a generic physio page with only a price line and two different phone numbers — [Nuffield The Holly](https://www.nuffieldhealth.com/physiotherapy/the-holly/mummy-mot)
- NHS Borders leaflet (Right Decisions): a model for a plain-language diastasis explainer with thresholds ("one to two fingerbreadths is common"; ">2 fingerbreadths + doming → follow-up") — [NHS Right Decisions](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/patient-information-leaflets/primary-community-services/physiotherapy/separation-of-the-abdominal-muscles-in-pregnancy-and-beyond)

**Australia**
- Sydney Physio Clinic, diastasis page: a question-led educational structure by a named clinician. It reframes "flat stomach" as function, mentions real-time ultrasound, and covers surgery only as a last resort — [Sydney Physio Clinic](https://www.sydneyphysioclinic.com.au/womens-health-physio/diastasis-recti/)
- Pogo Physio, Gold Coast: the warmest reassurance copy ("you can bring bub!", consult while holding the baby, towel-covered exam with an ultrasound alternative) and a 4-step consult explainer — [Pogo Physio](https://www.pogophysio.com.au/blog/womens-health-physiotherapy-consult/)

**USA**
- PhysioLogic, Brooklyn NYC: the clearest CTA rhythm ("Schedule Appointment" + "Request A Call" in 4 places), a "Commonly Helps With" checklist, clinician cards, reviews and a 5-question FAQ. It also states the timing as 6 weeks vaginal / 8–10 weeks cesarean and "6 weeks to 8+ years postpartum" — [PhysioLogic](https://physiologicnyc.com/physical-therapy-brooklyn-nyc/prenatal-postpartum-care/)

**Spain (no full clinic page fetched; documents and directories only)**
- Quirónsalud Unidad de Suelo Pélvico (Valle del Henares): names a distinct "Consulta de Valoración Postparto" focused on whether and how to return to physical activity (search summary) — [Quirónsalud PDF](https://www.quironsalud.com/valle-henares/es/cartera-servicios/rehabilitacion-medicina-deportiva-fisioterapia/unidad-suelo-pelvico.ficheros/3177173-Unidad%20Suelo%20P%C3%A9lvico.pdf)
- HM Hospitales pelvic-floor leaflet: transparent pricing for the first assessment and for sessions (search summary) — [HM Hospitales](https://docs3.hmhospitales.com/app/uploads/sites/52/2026/07/24150002/WEB-TRIPTICO-11x21cm_SUELO-PELVICO.pdf)
- Doctoralia directory pages show the price range Spanish women see (Madrid €50–120 per session; Barcelona first visits about €60–80) (search summary) — [Doctoralia Madrid](https://www.doctoralia.es/tratamientos-servicios/fisioterapia-del-postparto/madrid); [Doctoralia Barcelona](https://www.doctoralia.es/tratamientos-servicios/fisioterapia-del-postparto/barcelona)

### Inferences
- In the Portuguese pages sampled, the gaps that the best foreign pages fill are: "Antes de vir" practicalities (baby, clothing, duration), a named clinician with a photo, a working WhatsApp CTA as the main action, and evidence-cautious wording. Doing all four would put Fisio4you ahead of the Lisbon and Porto benchmarks, and there appeared to be no dedicated Algarve competitor page in the searches.

### Gaps
- No Algarve/Portimão example found. No full Spanish clinic page fetched (403 and directory-only results). No design gallery of women's-health clinic sites was reviewed within the tool budget.
- POGP (UK), APA (Australia) and APFisio (Portugal) public guidance on how to describe postnatal physio was not retrieved.
