## 1. Service-page elements that drive enquiries (clinician photo/credentials, real photos, video, FAQs, pricing, reviews, CTAs, sticky mobile bar)

### Takeaway
There is very little rigorous, healthcare-specific conversion data; the best evidence is NN/g usability research (real photos of real staff/premises get attention, stock photos get ignored; upfront prices, visible contact info, FAQs and third-party reviews build credibility) plus vendor A/B tests (mostly e-commerce) showing sticky mobile CTAs lift conversions roughly 8–25%, with the effect concentrated on mobile. Patient-survey data says people want prices and read reviews, but most numbers are US-centric, old or vendor-produced.

### Cited Findings

**Photos: real people and premises vs stock**
- NN/g eyetracking: users scrutinise "photos of products and real people (as opposed to stock photos of models)", and ignore stock photos of generic people and purely decorative images that add no content. NN/g recommends investing in real photo shoots. (Older study, around 2010; no date shown on the page.) — [NN/g, "Not All Images Are the Same"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/)
- NN/g credibility study (Singapore usability study, May 2016, Aurora Harley): under "comprehensive, correct, current content", sites should show the service being done and *the people who will do the work*, not generic images of finished results. — [NN/g, "Trustworthiness in Web Design: 4 Credibility Factors"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
- Find-a-doctor user research by a healthcare UX agency: a top-ranked WebMD listing scored almost perfectly, and the *only* user criticism was the lack of doctor photos. (Agency research, small qualitative study, not peer-reviewed.) — [OHO Interactive](https://www.oho.com/blog/designing-effective-online-doctor-finder-tools-hospitals-healthcare-and-insurance)
- Agency opinion (no data): low-resolution, poorly lit headshots can make patients see clinicians as less professional or trustworthy. — [TBH Creative](https://www.tbhcreative.com/blog/have-the-best-doctor-pages-on-hospital-websites/)

**Upfront disclosure: prices, contact, FAQs**
- NN/g "upfront disclosure" factor: show what the base cost includes and any extra fees (ranges are acceptable if the line items are visible); show contact info prominently (e.g. in the utility navigation); avoid forms or walls before users see value. Provide supporting content such as tips and FAQs. (2016) — [NN/g trustworthy design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
- Deloitte: 60% of patients say they are more likely to choose a provider that publishes its prices relative to the local market (US data; date of underlying survey not stated on the page). — [Deloitte Insights, health care pricing strategies](https://www2.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/health-care/health-care-pricing-strategies.html)
- US consumer survey: 7 in 10 consumers want to know provider prices, but more than half never thought of looking for them. — [TechTarget RevCycle](https://www.techtarget.com/revcyclemanagement/news/366601634/Patients-Want-Healthcare-Price-Transparency-But-Few-Seek-It-Out)
- AJMC interview study (2017): patients approve of price shopping in principle but report practical barriers to doing it. — [AJMC 2017](https://www.ajmc.com/journals/issue/2017/2017-vol23-n6/patients-views-on-price-shopping-and-price-transparency?p=1&rel=0)
- Plastic surgery study (PRS Global Open, Oct 2024): most patients said their final cost was close to, or below, the estimate they got online. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/6cbe42c4445e4bb2912b889e08584170)

**Reviews**
- BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2025 (published Jan 2025): consumers read both positive and negative reviews to form their own view; expectations about review count and recency have softened. — [BrightLocal 2025](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)
- BrightLocal 2026 (seen via secondary summary, not checked against the report): 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses; Google's share as a review source fell from 83% (2025) to 71%; Apple Maps rose from 14% to 27%; generative-AI tools became the third most popular source of business recommendations. — [BrightLocal 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/2026/)
- NN/g: users trust third-party reviews, social media and news more than content the company controls. Testimonials on a company's own site are viewed with scepticism, so a presence on external review sites matters more. (2016) — [NN/g trustworthy design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
- NRC Health (2015, old): 70% had viewed physician ratings or reviews online, but only a minority said reviews were their first step in choosing a physician. — [NRC Health 2015](https://nrchealth.com/2015-healthcare-consumer-trends)

**Video**
- JMIR 2020 study on primary-care physician online biographies: after participants read mostly negative reviews, a short video introduction produced a positive surprise and more favourable views of the provider. It measured *perceptions*, not bookings. (Read from a search summary, not the full paper.) — [JMIR 2020;22(4):e16635](https://www.jmir.org/2020/4/e16635/XML)
- Medical Economics (trade press) claims 84% of patients check a provider's online reputation before booking, and that practices with video were "53% more likely to rank first on Google". The second figure is an SEO claim with no stated methodology. Treat both as weak. — [Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/connect-patients-through-video)

**Sticky CTAs and CTA placement**
- Conversion Rate Experts tested four sticky-CTA variants. Adding the sticky button lifted conversions by at least 8%, and the best variant increased sales by 25%. — [Conversion Rate Experts win report](https://conversion-rate-experts.com/sticky-cta-win-report/)
- Zipify mobile split test: sticky button gave +10% add-to-carts and +9% conversions (95% confidence; e-commerce). — [Zipify](https://zipify.com/mobile-sticky-button-split-test-results/)
- Convertica e-commerce case study: the sticky CTA had a significant positive effect on mobile engagement and revenue but no notable change on desktop. — [Convertica](https://convertica.org/ecommerce-case-study-sticky-cta/)
- Homepage sticky CTA test (whisky subscription brand): +20.4% conversion, 95.1% probability to beat control. The authors advise sticky CTAs only when the page is long enough that visitors decide below the original CTA. — [Convertibles case study](https://convertibles.dev/blogs/case-studies/homepage-sticky-cta-case-study)
- Apparel brand sticky add-to-cart: +16.27% conversion rate. — [BlendCommerce](https://blendcommerce.com/blogs/ab-tests-shopify/adding-a-sticky-cta-to-the-product-detail-page)
- Conversion Sciences reports that a sticky header plus a follow-up CTA produced a "relatively large increase in phone calls" for a lead-gen client. This is the closest analogue to a call or WhatsApp bar, but no figures are given. — [Conversion Sciences](https://conversionsciences.com/mobile-ab-testing-guide-for-the-mobile-web/)
- NN/g guidance on persistent headers (Page Laubheimer, Apr 2021; design guidance, not a study): persistent elements cost screen space, which matters most on mobile. Keep them small (about 16pt text, tap targets at least about 1×1 cm), opaque and high-contrast, and with minimal motion. Consider "partially persistent" bars that reappear on a small scroll up (300–400 ms slide-in). Only make persistent the elements users need often. — [NN/g Sticky Headers](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sticky-headers/)

### Inferences
- For Fisio4you, the strongest evidence-backed trust levers are: (a) real photos of Mário and Angela at work in the real premises, which is already the site's direction, (b) visible prices or price ranges with what each one includes, (c) clinician credentials, which Portuguese law requires anyway (see section 4), (d) links to or embeds of third-party Google reviews rather than self-published testimonials, and (e) a short FAQ per service.
- A small sticky mobile bar with WhatsApp and Call is reasonable on long service pages. The evidence is mostly e-commerce and vendor-run, so the expected lift is directional (roughly high single digits to around 25%), not guaranteed. A partially persistent bar that hides on scroll down and shows on scroll up is a lower-cost compromise per NN/g.
- An intro video from the lead physiotherapist likely helps perception (JMIR). Booking impact is unproven, which is consistent with keeping videos click-to-play rather than autoplay.

### Gaps
- No Baymard or NN/g study specific to healthcare or clinic *service pages*, and no peer-reviewed data tying clinician photos, FAQs or prices to enquiry rates.
- No healthcare-specific A/B test isolating a sticky click-to-call or WhatsApp bar.
- No reliable data on the optimal number of CTAs per page. Vendor claims exist but have no methodology, so none are included.
- Most price and review data is US-based. No Portugal-specific patient survey on how people choose a physiotherapist online was found.
- Mobile share of web traffic in Portugal was not reported in DataReportal Digital 2026 Portugal.

## 2. WhatsApp click-to-chat as the primary CTA (Portugal, Spain, Brazil): usage, best practices, pitfalls

### Takeaway
WhatsApp is the most-used social or messaging platform in Portugal, used by roughly 90%+ of internet users, so a WhatsApp-first CTA fits local behaviour. There is no rigorous published data on website click-to-chat conversion or on prefilled messages; the best-practice advice is vendor opinion. Meta-commissioned surveys show a majority of consumers prefer messaging businesses, but country breakdowns for Portugal were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Statista (2024): WhatsApp was the most used social media platform in Portugal, used by over 90% of internet users. — [Statista, most used social media platforms Portugal 2024](https://statista.com/statistics/1373637/portugal-most-used-social-media-platforms)
- Marktest "Os Portugueses e as Redes Sociais" 2025 (press coverage): WhatsApp is among the networks with the highest penetration. Facebook regained the top spot for *notoriety*, followed by Instagram, WhatsApp, X and TikTok. — [Notícias ao Minuto](https://www.noticiasaominuto.com/tech/2872417/facebook-voltou-a-ser-a-rede-social-com-maior-notoriedade-em-portugal)
- DataReportal Digital 2026 Portugal (published 8 Nov 2025): 9.26M internet users (89.0% of the population, Oct 2025) and 13.9M cellular connections (133% of the population). The page contains no WhatsApp figure. — [DataReportal Digital 2026 Portugal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-portugal)
- DataReportal Digital 2025 Portugal: 9.27M internet users and 7.49M social media user identities (71.9% of the population) in Jan 2025. — [DataReportal Digital 2025 Portugal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-portugal)
- Meta-commissioned survey (more than 5,000 adults across markets incl. Germany, UK, Spain, India, Mexico and Brazil): 66% of adults globally message a business at least once a week; India was highest at 86%. The article is probably from 2022, so the data is older. — [Deccan Herald](https://deccanherald.com/business/technology/portion-of-online-adults-in-india-messaging-a-biz-at-least-once-a-week-highest-globally-metas-survey-1151531)
- Kantar survey for Meta (11,056 adults, 22 markets, Apr–Sep 2025), cited by a secondary vendor source: 73.3% of consumers prefer messaging when communicating with a business. Not verified against Meta's own report; no country breakdown. — [ChatMaxima (secondary)](https://chatmaxima.com/blog/?p=3556)
- Brazil (vendor blog, unsourced): 73% of online users find messaging quicker and easier for business communication. — [Gupshup](https://gupshup.io/resources/blog/business-of-whatsapp-in-brazil)
- Spain (2014, outdated): 95% of Spaniards prefer texting to talking. — [The Local Spain, 2014](https://www.thelocal.es/20140918/spaniards-would-rather-whatsapp-than-talk)
- Link mechanics: a click-to-chat link is `wa.me/<number>`, and a prefilled message is added with `?text=` plus URL-encoded text. — [u2l.ai wa.me guide (2026)](https://u2l.ai/blog/whatsapp-click-to-chat-link). WhatsApp's official help article on this ("Como usar a funcionalidade Clicar para conversar") is at [faq.whatsapp.com/5913398998672934](https://faq.whatsapp.com/5913398998672934); only its title could be retrieved.
- Vendor benchmarks (unsourced, treat as directional only): a floating WhatsApp widget gets 3–5% of visitors clicking, and an inline button 8–12% on the pages where it appears. — [Kanal](https://getkanal.com/blog/whatsapp-click-to-chat-link). A WhatsApp conversion rate of 5–15% is also claimed. — [Qualimero (DE)](https://qualimero.com/blog/whatsapp-link-erstellen)
- Opinion on prefilled text: many businesses write prefilled messages like a mini intake form, which usually hurts conversion. Name the specific topic or page and keep the message easy to send as-is. — [Hyperleap](https://hyperleap.ai/blog/create-whatsapp-link)
- Click-to-WhatsApp *ads* (not website buttons): Air France reported a 4.5× higher CTR for one message type. — [ActiveCampaign](https://www.activecampaign.com/blog/click-to-whatsapp-ads-guide)

### Inferences
- Prefill a short, natural, service-specific message in the page's language, e.g. PT "Olá, gostaria de marcar uma consulta de fisioterapia (dor lombar)." and EN "Hi, I'd like to book a physiotherapy appointment (lower back pain)." This gives the clinic context without making the patient fill in a form. Vendor advice is consistent with this, but it is not measured.
- Likely pitfalls (reasoned, not measured):
  - Desktop visitors without WhatsApp Desktop land on WhatsApp Web, so keep the phone number visible as text too.
  - Some tourists use other messengers, so offer the phone as a second option.
  - Patients may send health details over WhatsApp. Avoid asking for clinical details in the prefilled text and reply with minimal health data (GDPR).
  - Response-time expectations are high on messaging. If replies aren't quick, state opening hours or a typical reply time near the button.
- Track clicks per page with distinct prefilled text or UTM-style tags so the clinic can see which service pages generate conversations.
- Under Regulamento ERS 1058/2016 every health advert must include a contact for clarification (see section 4), and a WhatsApp or phone CTA can serve that purpose.

### Gaps
- No Meta or WhatsApp official statistics specific to Portugal for business messaging. Meta's "State of Business Messaging" report, which might have country breakdowns, was not found.
- The BCG Consumer Sentiment Survey 2025 figure (WhatsApp at about 90% penetration in Portugal) appeared only in a search summary; the primary source URL was not located.
- No controlled study on prefilled vs empty WhatsApp messages, or on WhatsApp vs call CTAs for healthcare.
- Statista's per-country WhatsApp penetration chart (Q2 2025) is paywalled; values for Portugal, Spain and Brazil are hidden.

## 3. Interactive elements (body-area selectors, symptom checkers, self-assessment checklists): engagement/conversion effects

### Takeaway
Research on digital body maps is clinical: they are valid and usable for patients reporting where it hurts, with known usability pitfalls (left/right confusion, back and shoulder areas, frail users). No marketing or conversion evidence was found for body-area selectors on clinic websites. Symptom checkers are inaccurate and risk-averse, and under Portuguese law anything resembling self-diagnosis or incitement to treatment without prior assessment is risky.

### Cited Findings
- CHOIR electronic body map (Scherrer et al., PAIN Reports, Jan 2021): valid and reliable for pain distribution. Agreement was weaker for the back and shoulders. Mismatches came from confusion over body regions and left/right orientation, and changing the instructions fixed them. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/760f6366131e44a38bccb84ff8518f3b); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7813550)
- Electronic Michigan Body Map (68 chronic pain patients): most patients placed their pain accurately on both paper and electronic versions, and most preferred an expanded electronic version. — [Regional Anesthesia & Pain Medicine](https://rapm.bmj.com/content/45/2/145)
- Computerised body map for advanced cancer patients: iterative usability testing made it usable even by frail patients. — [SINTEF](https://www.sintef.no/publikasjoner/publikasjon/1258791/)
- Manchester / uMotif Digital Pain Manikin for musculoskeletal pain: a feasibility study with about 100 patients on their own devices; validity still to be established. — [Med-Tech News](https://www.med-technews.com/news/Digital-in-Healthcare-News/study-examines-digital-body-mapping-tool-for-musculoskeletal/)
- Semigran et al., BMJ 2015 (23 symptom checkers, 45 vignettes): the correct diagnosis was listed first in 34% of evaluations and in the top 20 in 58%. Appropriate triage was given in 80% of emergent, 55% of non-emergent and 33% of self-care cases. Advice was generally risk-averse. (Older data.) — [BMJ 2015;351:h3480](https://www.bmj.com/node/900623); [HealthExec coverage](https://healthexec.com/node/70833)
- Legal context: DL 238/2015 art. 7(1)(b) and 7(2)(b) prohibit advertising that encourages buying health services without regard to need or to prior individual assessment or diagnosis, or that leads to unnecessary consumption or consumption without prior assessment by a qualified professional. — [DL 238/2015 (INFARMED consolidated)](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/1065790/011-O_DL_238_2015.pdf)

### Inferences
- A body-area figure works best as *navigation* ("Where does it hurt? → service page for back, knee, shoulder…"), not as diagnosis. Accessible text links should sit beside it, because the research shows left/right and back-of-body confusion. Label views clearly as front/back and the patient's left/right, and provide a plain list as an alternative for keyboard and screen-reader users.
- Avoid symptom checkers or quizzes that output a diagnosis or a recommended treatment. They are inaccurate (BMJ 2015) and could be read as inciting treatment without prior assessment (DL 238/2015 art. 7). A simple checklist of when to see a physiotherapist, or when to seek urgent care, framed as general information that ends in "book an assessment", is safer.

### Gaps
- No A/B or analytics evidence found on whether body-map selectors raise or lower enquiries on clinic or physio websites.
- No NN/g or Baymard article specific to body-figure navigation.
- No evidence on self-assessment checklists' effect on conversion.

## 4. Portuguese law on health advertising (DL 238/2015, Regulamento ERS 1058/2016, ERS guidance, Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas, Livro de Reclamações, phone-cost rule)

### Takeaway
A clinic website counts as health advertising in Portugal. Every service page must, directly or through a global block, carry mandatory identification:
- name or firm
- ERS registration number
- operating licence number, if applicable
- address
- for any named professional: their cédula profissional number and issuing body, plus their qualifications

It must also give a contact for clarification and state when a service needs prior assessment. Prices must state what is included and the total in euros. Prohibited: false guarantees of cure or results, "innovative" or "pioneering" claims without validation, inciting treatment without need or assessment, misleading "free", "discount" or "promo" wording, health services as contest prizes, and misuse of seals. Testimonials and before/after images are not banned outright: they must not create false or dangerous expectations and need patients' prior, explicit consent. Fines run up to €44,891.81 for companies.

### Cited Findings

**Scope (DL 238/2015, in force 1 Nov 2015)**
- Applies to health advertising by any public or private party about interventions for protecting or maintaining health or preventing or treating disease, "independentemente da forma ou meios". — [DL 238/2015, art. 1 (INFARMED consolidated text)](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/1065790/011-O_DL_238_2015.pdf); official: [Diário da República](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/decreto-lei/238-2015-70686132)
- "Prática de publicidade em saúde" includes any commercial communication *and* "a informação, ainda que sob a aparência, designadamente, de informação editorial, técnica ou científica" that directly or indirectly promotes health services. This means blog or educational content on a clinic site is covered. — [DL 238/2015, art. 2(b)](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/1065790/011-O_DL_238_2015.pdf)
- Reg. ERS 1058/2016 applies regardless of medium, "nomeadamente audiovisual, oral, escrito, internet ou outro". — [ERS explanatory note on Reg. 1058/2016](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/informacao-regulamento-ers-1058-2016.pdf)

**Principles (DL arts. 3–6)**
- Transparency, reliability and lawfulness; objectivity; scientific rigour (art. 3).
- The advertising must truthfully identify the provider. It must not create doubts about the services offered, agreements actually held, or *professionals' qualifications* (art. 4(1)–(2)).
- Advertising is unlawful if the provider is not registered with the ERS or lacks an operating licence where one is required (art. 4(3)). — [DL 238/2015](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/1065790/011-O_DL_238_2015.pdf)
- **Testimonials:** art. 5(3): the message "não deve conter expressões, conceitos, testemunhos ou afirmações que possam criar no utente expectativas potenciadoras de perigo ou potencialmente ameaçadoras para a sua integridade física ou moral". — [DL 238/2015](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/1065790/011-O_DL_238_2015.pdf)
- Art. 6: use only information accepted by the technical or scientific community, and avoid misleading users about the real usefulness of the service. — [DL 238/2015](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/1065790/011-O_DL_238_2015.pdf)

**Prohibited practices (DL art. 7; the list is illustrative, not exhaustive)**

The general rule is that any health advertising that misleads, or is likely to mislead, the user's decision is prohibited. Art. 7(1) names in particular:
- (a) hiding or misleading about a service's main characteristics, including technical or scientific claims without supporting evidence, or "expressões de inovação ou de pioneirismo" without prior evaluation by the competent bodies;
- (b) advising or inciting the purchase of services without regard to need, to their real properties, or to the need for prior individual assessment or diagnosis;
- (c) falsely referring to "demonstrações ou garantias de cura ou de resultados" or to absence of side effects;
- (d) confusion about the provider's identity, qualifications or licensing;
- (e) confusion with competitors' services, brands or skills, or an impression of quality through misuse of brands or distinctive seals or certifications;
- (f) calling a service "grátis", "gratuito", "sem encargos", "com desconto" or "promoção" when the user pays more than the unavoidable cost;
- (g) bait-and-switch pricing.

Art. 7(2) also prohibits:
- (a) harassment, coercion or undue influence;
- (b) inducing "consumo desnecessário, nocivo ou sem diagnóstico ou avaliação prévios por profissional habilitado";
- (c) offering health services as prizes or gifts in contests or draws.

Source: [DL 238/2015 art. 7](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/1065790/011-O_DL_238_2015.pdf); the ERS note confirms the list is not exhaustive ([ERS note on Reg. 1058/2016](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/informacao-regulamento-ers-1058-2016.pdf)).

**Sanctions (DL art. 8)**
- Breaches of arts. 3–6: €250–€3,740.98 for individuals, €1,000–€44,891.81 for companies.
- Breaches of art. 7: €250–€3,740.98 for individuals, €3,000–€44,891.81 for companies.
- Negligence is punishable, with limits halved.
- Accessory sanctions include seizure of materials and a temporary ban of up to two years on the professional or advertising activity.
- The ERS supervises and applies the fines. The Código da Publicidade and the unfair commercial practices regime apply subsidiarily (art. 9).

Source: [DL 238/2015](https://www.infarmed.pt/documents/15786/1065790/011-O_DL_238_2015.pdf)

**Mandatory content: Regulamento ERS 1058/2016 (published DR 2.ª série 24 Nov 2016, in force 24 Dec 2016)**

All quotations and article references in this block come from the [ERS note on Reg. 1058/2016](https://www.omd.pt/content/uploads/2017/12/informacao-regulamento-ers-1058-2016.pdf).

- Identification, art. 2(2): name or firm and commercial name; tax number (NIF). The NIF is waived when the advertiser is registered with the ERS (art. 2(4)).
- Identification for ERS-registered providers, art. 2(3):
  - (a) ERS registration number of the provider or of the establishment;
  - (b) operating licence number, where the activity type requires a licence;
  - (c) address or geographic location of the establishment;
  - (d) when the advertiser is a health professional: "número de cédula profissional e indicação das habilitações profissionais".
- Content required for complete information, art. 3(2):
  - (a) the establishment's address when advertising services provided there;
  - (b) for any identified or identifiable health professional, the cédula or professional card number *and the issuing body*;
  - (c) when mentioning insurance or agreements (convenções, acordos, protocolos), any restrictions and where the full terms can be consulted;
  - (d) when prices are shown, which acts are included and the total price in euros including taxes;
  - (e) when using "grátis", "desconto", "promoção" and similar, exactly which acts are covered;
  - (f) when invoking technical or scientific characteristics, the technical or scientific source *and* the national or international norms or guidelines followed;
  - (g) when a service needs prior assessment, individual diagnosis or medical prescription, this must be stated expressly;
  - (h) time limits for limited-duration campaigns.
- Art. 3(3): every advert, in any medium, must include a contact for user clarification.
- Art. 3(6): do not use brands or signs that could cause confusion with a competitor.
- **Language**, art. 3(4)–(5): advertising must be in Portuguese. Foreign-language terms are allowed if commonly used in health. Foreign languages *are permitted*, including alongside Portuguese, when the advertising is aimed exclusively or mainly at foreigners.
- **Legibility**, art. 4: minimum character size of 9 points for written media "incluindo imprensa, internet e correio eletrónico". Internet messages must stay visible long enough to be read or heard.
- Violations are punished under DL 238/2015 art. 8(1)(a) (art. 5 of the Regulation).

**ERS enforcement and guidance**
- ERS sanction decisions repeatedly target adverts that omit the SRER registration number and the licence number (citing art. 2(3)(a)–(b) of the Regulation), including an SMS campaign without the ERS number. (Seen via search summaries of ERS decision PDFs; the PDFs could not be opened.) — [ERS vnc-pt 183/2024](https://ers.pt/media/vxleaomk/vnc-pt_183_2024-pco_027_2023.pdf); [ERS vnc-pt 146/2024](https://www.ers.pt/media/frccgt1w/vnc-pt_146_2024-pco_253_2023.pdf); [ERS leaflet "práticas publicitárias em saúde"](https://ers.pt/media/x3enq5ls/triptico_praticas-publicitarias-em-saude_web.pdf)
- ERS Manual de Boas Práticas de Publicidade em Saúde, as summarised by the Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas (post dated 12 Feb 2026, updated 7 Mar 2026):
  - advertising must be ethical, truthful, transparent and user-centred, and distinguishable from editorial content;
  - the professional and the establishment must be clearly identified;
  - prohibited practices include guarantees of results, improper use of titles, and advertising services not provided;
  - **testimonials and "before and after" images must be clear and must not create false expectations, and publishing patients' photos or testimonies requires their prior, free and explicit consent (image rights and personal data).**

ERS FAQ: [ERS FAQ publicidade em saúde](https://www.ers.pt/pt/prestadores/perguntas-frequentes/faqs/perguntas-frequentes-sobre-publicidade-em-saude/)
- The ERS created a Health Advertising Observatory, the Observatório da Publicidade em Saúde (OPS), referenced in Nota Informativa 1/2026, and the Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas sits on its Strategic Council. Advertising enforcement is an active 2026 priority. — [ERS Nota Informativa 1/2026](https://www.ers.pt/media/1izlw3hc/nota-informativa_1_2026_pub.pdf) (search snippet only); [Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas](https://ordemdosfisioterapeutas.pt/en/2026/02/12/)

**Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas**
- Per the Ordem, physiotherapy advertising must follow the legal framework *and* the Code of Ethics (Código Deontológico), including honesty, professional competence and placing the user's interests above commercial ones (Article 19, Chapter XVI). — [Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas, Feb 2026](https://ordemdosfisioterapeutas.pt/en/2026/02/12/)
- The Ordem's statute is Lei 122/2019, amended by Lei 71/2023. The Ordem must ensure compliance with the code of ethics. The draft code went to public consultation in 2023 (Aviso n.º 3738/2023). — [Lei 122/2019](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei/122-2019-125016439); [Lei 71/2023](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei/71-2023-229915927); [Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas, Feb 2023 consultation](https://ordemdosfisioterapeutas.pt/en/2023/02/)
- The Ordem's 2026 activity plan includes drafting guidance to enforce the code, so further advertising guidance may come. — [Plano de actividades 2026](https://ordemdosfisioterapeutas.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Plano-de-actividades-2026_21102025.pdf)

**Livro de Reclamações (electronic complaints book)**
- DL 74/2017 created the electronic complaints book (LRE). Providers with a website must publicise access to the LRE platform "de forma visível e destacada"; those without a website must have an email address to receive complaints. — [Garrigues](https://www.garrigues.com/pt/pt-PT/news/obrigatoriedade-de-dispor-de-um-livro-de-reclamacoes-eletronico)
- Private, social and cooperative health providers regulated by the ERS have been required to register on the LRE platform since December 2019. The electronic book does not replace the paper book at the premises; both are required. The ERS's 2024 supervisory alert reminds providers they must reply to complainants within 10 working days. (Seen via search summaries of ERS PDFs.) — [ERS Alerta de Supervisão 05/2024](https://ers.pt/media/fuam0eaw/alerta_supervisao_05_2024_lre.pdf); [ERS "Livro de reclamações: quem tem de ter"](https://www.ers.pt/media/2881/livro-de-reclamações-quem-tem-de-ter_ers-3.pdf)

**Phone-number cost labelling (DL 59/2021, amended by Lei 14/2023)**
- Businesses showing a contact phone number must indicate the call cost. Standard labels such as "Chamada para a rede fixa nacional" or "Chamada para rede móvel nacional" may be used. After the 2023 amendment, the duty is limited to the supplier's *website* and written contracts, and breaches became minor offences. — [Garrigues on DL 59/2021](https://www.garrigues.com/pt/pt-PT/news/decreto-lei-no-592021-regula-o-regime-aplicavel-disponibilizacao-e-divulgacao-linhas); [APCMC on the 2023 amendment](https://apcmc.pt/legislacao/linhas-telefonicas-para-contacto-do-consumidor-publicada-alteracao-ao-dl-59-2021/); [AMT summary](https://www.amt-autoridade.pt/media/3128/linhas_telefonicas_consumidor.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Practical compliance checklist for each service page.** This is a reading of the sources above, not legal advice.
  1. A global footer, visible on every page in both languages, showing:
     - legal name or firm and commercial name "Fisio4you";
     - "Registo ERS n.º …";
     - "Licença de funcionamento n.º …", if physiotherapy at this establishment type requires one (to verify);
     - full address in Portimão;
     - phone with "(Chamada para a rede móvel/fixa nacional)" next to it;
     - Livro de Reclamações Eletrónico link and icon, displayed prominently. The repo already has `public/brand/livro-reclamacoes.webp`.
  2. Wherever Mário or Angela are named or shown: their cédula profissional number, "Ordem dos Fisioterapeutas" as issuing body, and their qualifications.
  3. On each service page, a line such as "Requer avaliação prévia por fisioterapeuta" / "An initial assessment by a physiotherapist is required", per Reg. art. 3(2)(g).
  4. If prices are shown: what the session or assessment includes, the total in € with VAT, and any packages spelled out.
  5. Avoid all of the following:
     - "garantimos resultados", "cura", "sem dor garantido";
     - "o melhor / n.º 1 do Algarve";
     - "pioneiro", "tecnologia inovadora" or "o primeiro";
     - "grátis" or "promoção" without precise terms;
     - Instagram giveaways offering sessions as prizes.
  6. Any evidence claim, e.g. "shockwave therapy is proven to…", should cite the source or guideline.
  7. Testimonials and Google review embeds are allowed if truthful and not promising outcomes, and only with documented explicit consent for anything self-published. Before/after images are high-risk for a physio clinic and are best avoided.
- **Bilingual EN pages are expressly permitted** because foreigners are a main audience (Reg. art. 3(5)). The Portuguese version remains the default, and both language versions must carry the mandatory identification elements.
- **Superlatives and comparisons** ("o melhor", "líder") are not named explicitly in DL 238/2015. They are likely caught by the general misleading rule in art. 7(1), the confusion-with-competitors rule in art. 7(1)(e), and the subsidiary application of the Código da Publicidade (art. 9). This is an inference; no ERS decision on superlatives was retrieved.
- Educational blog posts and FAQ content are also "health advertising" under art. 2(b), so the same rules on scientific rigour and identification apply to them.

### Gaps
- The full text of the ERS Manual de Boas Práticas (date and version unknown, possibly updated in 2026) and ERS Nota Informativa 1/2026 could not be read because ers.pt was unreachable. Their specific wording on testimonials, before/after images, influencers, social media and superlatives is known only second-hand.
- The final approved Código Deontológico dos Fisioterapeutas (DR reference) and the exact text of its Article 19 / Chapter XVI on advertising were not retrieved.
- Whether a physiotherapy clinic in Portimão requires an operating licence (licença de funcionamento) under the current licensing regime, and therefore whether a licence number must be shown, was not verified.
- No ERS decision specifically about superlatives, comparative claims or before/after images was retrieved. One search result mentioned a fine of about €6,000 in an ERS case, but it could not be tied to a specific document, so it is excluded.
- It was not checked whether DL 238/2015 has been amended since the 2016 INFARMED consolidation. No amendment surfaced in searches, but the current consolidated version on Diário da República should be checked.

### Inferences
- The recurring strengths of the recognised clinic sites are:
  - a warm-but-premium tone (Dębski's "prestigious yet close" brief matches Fisio4you's institutional-but-human direction);
  - strong typography and real photography;
  - live third-party reviews (101 Physio's automatic Google Reviews, consistent with NN/g's finding that third-party reviews beat self-published testimonials);
  - service content deep enough to rank in search.
- Gallery awards reward visual craft, not conversion. None of these entries publishes conversion data, so they are visual references only.
